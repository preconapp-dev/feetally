How Vrbo fees work

Vrbo, part of Expedia Group, is the second-largest vacation rental marketplace in the United States and lists whole homes rather than shared rooms. Most US hosts use the pay-per-booking model, where Vrbo takes a cut of each reservation instead of an annual listing fee.

Vrbo's help center now describes the pay-per-booking fee as a flat, all-in 12% commission that includes payment processing fees. It is charged on the rental amount and any fees you charge the traveler, such as cleaning, pet and boat fees. Taxes and security deposits are treated differently: they carry a 3% payment processing fee but not the 12% commission. Bookings that come through Vrbo's expanded distribution partners may carry higher fees.

This is a big change. For years the pay-per-booking commission was 5% plus 3% payment processing, so hosts paid 8% in total. Vrbo's help page gives no effective date; Skift reported on September 29, 2026 that the flat 12% rate applies from October 29, 2026 to all hosts and property managers, including those on the legacy annual subscription. The calculator lets you pick either schedule so you can check a booking made before or after the switch.

  • Commission: a flat, all-in 12% of the rental amount plus any fees you charge the traveler (cleaning, pet, boat). Vrbo's help center says the 12% includes payment processing fees. On the legacy schedule this was a 5% commission with 3% processing on top.
  • Payment processing: included in the 12% on the rental amount and your fees. A separate 3% payment processing fee applies only to taxes and security deposits, so a $1,000 rental with $100 of tax pays $120.00 plus $3.00.
  • Taxes and security deposits: subject to the 3% payment processing fee but not the commission. Where VAT applies, it is charged in addition to the 12% commission.
  • Not charged on: Property Damage Protection and Trip Cancellation Protection (travel insurance) sold to the traveler.
  • Property management software: hosts who connect through a PMS are charged the 12% commission on bookings made through Vrbo's sites (12%–15% in some regions such as Europe, Australia and New Zealand). Reservations that come from Vrbo but are not booked through its checkout carry a 10% booking fee on the rental amount, mandatory fees and withheld deposits, excluding taxes and refundable deposits.
  • Traveler service fee: Vrbo also charges the guest a service fee at checkout, a percentage of the reservation total before taxes and refundable fees that varies with the reservation amount. Hosts cannot waive or reduce it. It does not reduce your payout, but it raises the total the guest sees.

Ways to keep more of each Vrbo sale

  • Fold the fee into your nightly rate rather than the cleaning fee. Both are commissionable, so a higher cleaning fee does not reduce what Vrbo takes.
  • Collect security deposits only if you need them. Vrbo's help center says deposits carry the 3% payment processing fee.
  • If you list on both Vrbo and Airbnb, compare payouts on the same subtotal: Vrbo's all-in 12% is below Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee, but Vrbo guests also pay a service fee, so compare what the guest pays as well as what you keep.
  • Check your Owner Dashboard for the rate on your listing. Vrbo says distribution-partner bookings and some regions carry higher fees than the standard 12%.
  • Use the target-profit field to price from your costs up. A $500 profit target on an $80-cost stay needs about $659 in rent on the 12% schedule.

Vrbo fee FAQ

What percentage does Vrbo take from hosts?

Vrbo's help center lists a flat, all-in 12% commission on pay-per-booking listings, which it says includes payment processing, plus a 3% payment processing fee on taxes and security deposits. Before the 2026 change hosts paid a 5% commission plus 3% processing, 8% in total.

Is the 3% payment processing fee in addition to the 12% commission?

Not on the rental amount. Vrbo's help center describes the 12% as a flat, all-in commission that includes payment processing fees. The 3% payment processing fee applies to taxes and security deposits, which are not subject to the commission.

Does Vrbo charge fees on taxes and damage deposits?

Only the 3% payment processing fee. Taxes and security deposits are not subject to the 12% commission.

Does Vrbo charge commission on cleaning fees?

Yes. The commission applies to the rental amount and any fees you charge the traveler, including cleaning, pet and boat fees.

Does Vrbo still offer an annual subscription instead of commission?

Vrbo has offered a legacy annual subscription for some hosts, but Skift reports those hosts move to the 12% pay-per-booking rate from October 29, 2026. Vrbo's subscription help page was unavailable when we checked, so we do not list a price.

When did Vrbo change to a 12% commission?

Vrbo's help center shows the 12% rate without a date. Skift reported on September 29, 2026 that it takes effect October 29, 2026 for all hosts and property managers, replacing the 5% paid by property managers on connected software and the 8% paid by other hosts.

How much does Vrbo take on a $1,000 booking?

$120.00 on the flat 12% schedule, plus 3% of any taxes or security deposit collected. On the legacy schedule (5% commission plus 3% processing) it is $80.00.

Good to know

Fee percentages are from Vrbo's help article "About the pay-per-booking (PPB) model", read 2026-10-11, which lists a flat, all-in 12% commission that includes payment processing fees, and a 3% payment processing fee on taxes and security deposits. Vrbo revised the article after we first read it on 2026-09-29, when it was titled "About pay-per-booking fees"; until 2026-10-11 this page added 3% processing on top of the 12% on the rental amount, and the calculator now follows the current wording. The article gives no effective date; the October 29, 2026 date and the previous 5%/8% rates come from a Skift news report of 2026-09-29, not from Vrbo. The current article no longer says that processing on a refunded deposit is reimbursed, and Vrbo's help article on fees for taxes and deposits returned an error when checked, so that statement was removed. Vrbo's help pages for the pay-per-booking overview and the annual subscription also returned an error, so the subscription price is not listed here. The traveler service fee is not modeled because Vrbo says only that the percentage varies with the reservation amount. Payout timing is not modeled.
