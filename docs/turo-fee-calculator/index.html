How Turo fees work

Turo is the largest peer-to-peer car sharing marketplace in the United States. Hosts list their own cars, set a daily price and availability, and guests book through the app. Turo handles payment, verification and protection, then pays the host a share of the trip price.

Turo does not charge a flat commission. Instead you pick an earnings plan (called protection plans until March 31, 2026), and the plan sets your host share, the percentage of the trip price you keep. For trips booked on or after January 7, 2026 there are three US plans: More peace of mind pays 70% with a $250 damage responsibility, Balanced pays 80% with $1,500, and More earnings pays 90% with $2,750. Turo keeps the rest, 30% down to 10%. Damage responsibility is the amount you cover yourself when the car has eligible damage on a trip.

The share applies to the trip price, which is what you charge for the days the car is booked. On trips booked on or after October 8, 2026, delivery fees and Extras use the same share as your plan. In nine markets (Austin, Dallas, Detroit, Las Vegas, Maui, Philadelphia, Phoenix, San Diego and Seattle) the share also moves with how far ahead the guest books: 65–80% on More peace of mind, 75–90% on Balanced and 85–100% on More earnings. This calculator uses the standard 70%, 80% and 90%.

  • More peace of mind plan: you keep 70% of the trip price and Turo keeps 30%. Damage responsibility is $250, the lowest of the three, and you get two extra days to submit additional distance and refueling reimbursements.
  • Balanced plan: you keep 80%, Turo keeps 20%. Damage responsibility is $1,500, with one extra day to submit those reimbursements.
  • More earnings plan: you keep 90%, Turo keeps 10%. Damage responsibility is $2,750, the highest of the three.
  • What the share applies to: the trip price (what you charge for the days your car is booked), delivery fees and Extras. Turo moved delivery fees and Extras, such as car seats and prepaid refuel, to the same share as your earnings plan for new trips booked on or after October 8, 2026; existing trips and extensions were not affected.
  • Variable host share: in Austin, Dallas, Detroit, Las Vegas, Maui, Philadelphia, Phoenix, San Diego and Seattle, your share depends on booking lead time, within 65–80% (More peace of mind), 75–90% (Balanced) and 85–100% (More earnings). Trips booked further ahead generally earn more and last-minute bookings can earn less. Turo says More earnings hosts in these markets can capture 100% of the trip price on trips booked 28 or more days in advance.
  • What the plans cover: each includes up to $750,000 in third-party liability insurance in most states, and Turo reimburses eligible damage above your damage responsibility up to the car's actual cash value or $200,000, whichever is less. Wear and tear, lost hosting income and a replacement vehicle during repairs are not covered.
  • No separate listing fee: the only platform cost on a standard trip is the share Turo keeps under your plan.

Ways to keep more of each Turo sale

  • Run the numbers on Balanced and More earnings side by side. On a $300 trip the difference is $30, but damage responsibility rises from $1,500 to $2,750, so the extra 10% only pays off over many claim-free trips.
  • Price your daily rate so the plan share still leaves room for cleaning, mileage wear and depreciation; no plan reimburses wear and tear. Use the target-profit field to work backward from what a trip needs to earn.
  • Reprice delivery and Extras if you are on More peace of mind or Balanced. On trips booked on or after October 8, 2026 those charges pay your plan's share, 70% or 80%, the same as the trip price.
  • In a variable-share market, encourage early bookings. Trips booked further in advance generally earn a higher share, and last-minute bookings can earn less than the standard rate.
  • You can switch plans at any time, but the change only applies to future trips, not ones already booked, so choose before your busy season fills up.

Turo fee FAQ

What percentage does Turo take from hosts?

Between 10% and 30% of the trip price in most US markets, depending on your earnings plan: 10% on More earnings, 20% on Balanced and 30% on More peace of mind. In nine markets the share varies with how far ahead the guest books.

What are Turo's earnings plans?

For trips booked on or after January 7, 2026 there are three: More peace of mind (keep 70%, $250 damage responsibility), Balanced (80%, $1,500) and More earnings (90%, $2,750). They replaced the older 60, 75, 80, 85 and 90 plans, which still apply to trips booked before that date.

Does Turo take a cut of delivery fees and Extras?

Yes. On trips booked on or after October 8, 2026, delivery fees and Extras such as car seats and prepaid refuel use the same share as your earnings plan, so in most markets you keep 70%, 80% or 90% of them.

Does Turo charge a monthly or listing fee?

No. Turo's cost to hosts is the share of each trip it keeps under your earnings plan. There is no subscription for a standard listing.

Is the plan share charged on the guest's fees too?

No. Fees the guest pays to Turo, such as the guest trip fee and the guest's protection plan, go to Turo. Your share is calculated on the trip price, delivery fees and Extras.

Can I change my Turo earnings plan?

Yes. Turo says you can choose a new plan at any time, and the change applies only to future trips, not trips that are already booked.

How much does a host make on a $300 Turo trip?

$210.00 on More peace of mind, $240.00 on Balanced and $270.00 on More earnings, before your own costs.

Good to know

Plan names, host shares and damage responsibility amounts are from Turo's US 'Earnings Plans for Turo Hosts' and 'Insurance & protection' pages, read 2026-10-10. The plans page says it covers trips booked on or after January 7 without a year; Turo's archived PDF of the five earlier plans (60, 75, 80, 85 and 90) is titled 'available before 1/7/26', which dates the change to January 7, 2026. Turo's Host Hub posts give 'March 31' (earnings plan renaming and variable host share) and 'October 8' (delivery fees and Extras) without a year; the posts are marked updated June 15, 2026 and October 1, 2026. Turo's help-center articles still show summaries of the five earlier plans to automated readers and their full text could not be read. Variable host share is published only as a range per plan, so it is not modeled; hosts in the nine listed markets should treat the result as the standard rate. Delivery fees and Extras are not separate inputs: add them to the trip price for trips booked on or after October 8, 2026 (before that, Turo's 2021 announcement put US hosts at 90% of delivery earnings). New York vehicles get state minimum liability coverage unless the host opts into More peace of mind for a $300,000 combined limit. Guest-side fees and payout timing are not modeled.
