How Twitch fees work

Twitch is the largest live-streaming platform for gaming, music, art, and chat. Streamers earn from paid subscriptions (Tier 1, 2, and 3), gifted subs, Bits cheered in chat, and ads that run on the stream. Affiliates and Partners share that money with Twitch.

The headline number is subscriptions. Twitch's baseline split is 50/50 on net subscription revenue, and Twitch says the vast majority of streamers are on those terms. The Plus Program raises your share: keep 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months and you move to 60/40 on paid and gifted subs; reach 300 Plus Points and you move to 70/30. Tier 1 subs are worth 1 point, Tier 2 subs 2 points, and Tier 3 subs 6 points. Since January 2024 there is no $100K cap on the 70/30 level.

Ads are separate. Twitch pays creators 55% of the revenue for each ad that runs on the stream, available to Partners through the Ads Incentive Program and to Affiliates who set Ads Manager to at least three minutes of ads per hour. Bits pay a flat 1 cent per Bit cheered.

One thing to keep in mind: the sub split applies to net revenue, which is what remains after Twitch's own deductions such as payment processing and taxes, so the dollars you see per sub are somewhat less than half the sticker price.   • Standard subscription split: Twitch keeps 50% of net revenue from paid and gifted subscriptions. This is the baseline for Affiliates and Partners; Twitch says the vast majority of streamers have these terms.
  • Plus Program 60/40: streamers who maintain 100 Plus Points for three consecutive months qualify for a 60/40 split on paid and gift subs, so Twitch's share drops to 40%.
  • Plus Program 70/30: maintaining 300 Plus Points (lowered from 350 in January 2024) for three consecutive months earns a 70/30 split; Twitch keeps 30%. The US$100K cap on net revenue at this level was removed in January 2024.
  • Plus Points: Tier 1 subs count as 1 point, Tier 2 subs as 2 points, and Tier 3 subs as 6 points toward the 100 and 300 thresholds. Twitch's October 2023 post says the higher split runs for 12 months once earned.
  • Ads: creators receive 55% of the revenue for each ad that runs on their stream, so Twitch keeps 45%. Partners access this through the Ads Incentive Program; Affiliates by running at least three minutes of ads per hour in Ads Manager.
  • Bits: Twitch pays 1 cent per Bit used to Cheer on your channel. Viewers pay more than a cent per Bit when they buy them, so Twitch's margin sits on the purchase side, not on your payout.
  • Net revenue: Twitch's sub splits apply to net subscription revenue, after Twitch's deductions for things like payment processing and taxes. Prime Gaming subs moved to a fixed-rate payout model on June 3, 2024.

Ways to keep more of each Twitch sale

  • Chase Plus Points, not just sub counts. A Tier 3 sub is worth 6 points, so 17 Tier 3 subs get you to the 100-point 60/40 level as fast as 100 Tier 1 subs.
  • Hold the level for three months. The Plus Program checks for three consecutive months at or above the threshold, so a one-month spike from a subathon does not qualify on its own.
  • Turn on ads if you are an Affiliate. Setting Ads Manager to three or more minutes per hour unlocks the 55% ad share and removes pre-rolls for your viewers.
  • Encourage subs over small Bit cheers for recurring support. A $5 tip in Bits nets you $5 worth of Bits at 1 cent each only if the viewer cheered 500 Bits, which cost them more than $5 to buy.
  • Track your effective rate, not the headline split. Because the split applies to net revenue, compare your actual monthly sub payout to your sub count to see what each sub is really worth to you.

Twitch fee FAQ

How much does Twitch take from subscriptions?

50% of net subscription revenue for most streamers. Plus Program streamers keep 60% (100 Plus Points) or 70% (300 Plus Points) instead, with no revenue cap on the 70/30 level since January 2024.

How do I get the 70/30 split on Twitch?

Maintain 300 Plus Points for three consecutive months. Tier 1 subs count 1 point, Tier 2 subs 2 points, and Tier 3 subs 6 points. At 100 points for three months you get 60/40 first.

How much is a Bit worth to a streamer?

1 cent. Twitch pays Affiliates and Partners a share of Bits revenue equal to $0.01 per Bit used to Cheer on their channel, so 1,000 Bits pays $10.

What is Twitch's ad revenue share?

Creators get 55% of the revenue for each ad that runs on their stream. Partners use the Ads Incentive Program; Affiliates qualify by running at least three minutes of ads per hour.

Is there still a $100K cap on the Twitch Plus Program?

No. Twitch removed the US$100K cap on net revenue at the 70/30 level for all streamers in January 2024.

Does Twitch take 50% of a $5.99 sub?

Twitch takes 50% of the net revenue from the sub, which is the price minus Twitch's deductions such as payment processing and taxes, so your payout is a bit under half the sticker price on the standard split.

Do Affiliates and Partners get different sub splits?

Both start at the standard 50/50. The Plus Program's 60/40 and 70/30 levels are earned by Plus Points, not by Partner status.

Good to know

Twitch's splits apply to net subscription revenue as Twitch defines it; the exact deductions between the sub price and net revenue are not published on the pages read, so enter a net figure for the most accurate result. Current US Tier 1, 2, and 3 sub prices, the Prime Gaming fixed payout rate, and payout minimums and timing were not verified from an official page for this calculator. The 12-month duration of a Plus level comes from Twitch's October 2023 Partner Plus post. Regional and legacy Partner contracts may differ.
